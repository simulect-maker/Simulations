Core Scientific Definitions

Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff): The net, actual positive charge experienced by an electron in a multi-electron atom. It is always lower than the actual atomic number (Z) because shielding core electrons screen the nucleus.
Shielding Effect (Screening Effect): The process by which core or inner-shell electrons act as an electrostatic barrier. By repelling outer-shell electrons, they decrease the overall attractive hold that the positively charged nucleus has on them.

Orbital Shielding Order & (n, l) Effects

Shielding Strength Order: s > p > d > f
The Penetration & Azimuthal (l) Effect: s-orbitals have a higher probability density near the nucleus (greater penetration power) and lower energy, allowing them to block outer attraction exceptionally well. d and f-orbitals are diffuse and split with many nodes, making them poor shielders.
The Principal (n) Effect: Inner shell core electrons (lower n) sit closer to the core and shield outermost shells with maximum efficiency (often counted as 1.0 on Slater's scales), whereas same-shell valence electrons offer weak, imperfect shielding.
